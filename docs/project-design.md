## Why this signal

**Recommend FEDFUNDS as the first hypothesis.** The Federal Funds Effective Rate is a monthly average of daily overnight rates. BIL holds short Treasury bills, so rising short rates can improve the carry available in the defensive sleeve. A limited shift preserves substantial global equity exposure. FEDFUNDS is observable in ALFRED, and Lab 17 showed exactly why its observation month is different from its availability date. [FRED series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/FEDFUNDS) · [ALFRED vintages](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/series?seid=FEDFUNDS) · [BIL holdings objective](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/individual/etfs/state-street-spdr-bloomberg-1-3-month-t-bill-etf-bil) · [VT holdings objective](https://advisors.vanguard.com/investments/products/vt/vanguard-total-world-stock-etf)

The weakness is substantial. An observed increase can already be priced in, and hikes can coincide with strong growth. U.S. monetary policy is also only one driver of a global equity portfolio. Research on stock reactions distinguishes policy surprises from observed rate changes. The 25 bp trigger is an economic convention chosen before testing, without optimizing it against results. [Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco research summary](https://www.frbsf.org/economic-research/publications/economic-letter/2003/05/finance-and-macroeconomics/)

**Student decision.** Use a 10-point reduction in equity exposure during tightening, from 50% BIL / 50% VT to 60% BIL / 40% VT. The student initially proposed 20 points and chose 10 after seeing that the larger shift missed the 6.5% estimated return hurdle. The smaller tilt aims to soften equity losses while preserving return potential and capacity to trade within the annual turnover limit. The test asks whether the observed rate change is early enough to help. Strong earnings and growth can sustain equity gains despite higher rates. Balance-sheet expansion can also weaken the broad tightening rationale. Track those as explanations and monitoring flags.

**Next-meeting expectations are a separate hypothesis.** A forward-looking trigger would require historical, timestamped, futures-implied probabilities available at each decision cutoff and a pre-specified probability threshold. Adding that trigger with an OR condition changes the rule being tested. The proposed primary rule uses observed ALFRED rates only. Policy rates and balance-sheet runoff are distinct tools, so a rate rise does not establish that quantitative tightening is occurring. [CME FedWatch methodology](https://www.cmegroup.com/articles/2023/understanding-the-cme-group-fedwatch-tool-methodology.html) · [Federal Reserve policy normalization](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/policy-normalization.htm)

Two credible alternatives would answer different questions. The 10-year minus 3-month Treasury spread has a documented connection to U.S. recession risk over roughly a year. Its horizon is a weak fit for a one-month allocation switch. VT trend directly measures the exposure being reduced and avoids macro revisions, but it needs a separate rule and can lose money by selling after declines and reentering after rebounds. Keep both out of the primary run. Register any future test separately with fresh evidence. [New York Fed on the term spread](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/capital_markets/ycfaq)

## Proposed rule card

1. **Neutral comparison.** Use 50% BIL and 50% VT as a fixed, pre-specified neutral weight for this experiment. It isolates timing from portfolio optimization. Compare the app's static portfolios separately with four-fund equal weight and inverse volatility as well as the policy reference.
2. **Trigger.** Calculate the difference between the latest eligible monthly FEDFUNDS observation and the observation three calendar months earlier in the same ALFRED vintage. If the change is at least +0.25 percentage points, target 60% BIL / 40% VT. Otherwise target 50% BIL / 50% VT. Apply the same limits to the static and placebo comparisons. IEF and LQD remain candidates for the static constrained search.
3. **Knowability.** For the last trading session of each month, use only an ALFRED snapshot requested as of the prior calendar date. Check its effective vintage, observation dates, and both rate values. Trade at the decision session close in the model and apply the weights to the subsequent close-to-close monthly return. Verify the monthly price labels correspond to the last trading close. If signal data are missing or chronologically invalid, log the issue and hold positions rather than feeding the strategy a fabricated signal.
4. **Costs.** Charge 10 basis points per 100% one-way turnover as a transparent assumption, applied symmetrically to the strategy, static comparison and placebo. One-way turnover is half the sum of absolute weight changes from drifted weights. A full 10-point transfer costs 1 bp of portfolio value. An exact entry and exit would use 20 percentage points of turnover before drift. The 25% calendar-year cap can still limit repeated trades or rebalancing. Report gross and net compounded returns. Fund expenses are already reflected in reported adjusted price returns, subject to source verification. Test 25 bp and a longer release lag as labeled sensitivities, without retuning the primary rule.
5. **Placebo.** Use the same 60% BIL / 40% VT defensive target when the rate change is at most −0.25 points. Keep weights, timing, costs and turnover conventions identical. Record trigger counts. The flipped rule can have different time in defense, so report average equity weight alongside both results.
6. **Holdout.** Propose development from October 2021 through September 2025 and a single untouched rule evaluation from October 2025 through September 2026. Carry end-of-development positions into the holdout. The returns already appear in retrospective Lab 15 inputs, so label this a historical holdout for the pre-specified rule, with no claim of unseen market history or prospective results. Keep the holdout sliced out during development, commit and push a frozen rule and data identifiers before running it, and log all attempted rules. Require new evidence for any post-result amendment.
7. **Decision gate.** Reject the overlay if holdout net return trails the neutral portfolio or the flipped-signal test, or if max drawdown worsens against neutral. If the overlay otherwise passes but requests the defensive target in fewer than two holdout months or never changes an actual holding relative to neutral, classify the evidence as inconclusive. Even a pass supports only a restricted further pilot, pending prospective evidence, client approval, and complete mandate checks.

For illustration, if the latest eligible rate is 4.50% and the rate three observation months earlier is 4.25%, the rule requests 60% BIL / 40% VT for the next holding period. If equity beats bills by 5 percentage points in that period, a full 10-point defensive tilt gives up about 0.50 percentage points before costs, ignoring drift and any turnover cap. If bills beat equity by 5 points, the impact reverses.

**Why 60/40.** On the prior Lab 15 estimates, the neutral 50/50 mix gives 7.74% estimated return and 7.52% estimated volatility. The defensive 60/40 mix gives 6.91% and 6.04%, respectively. It retains 40% in global equities and clears the 6.5% estimate hurdle while reducing modeled volatility by about 1.48 percentage points. The larger 70/30 tilt estimates 6.08% return. Even 65% BIL estimates 6.4996%, just below the unrounded hurdle. These are static estimates. The holdout will test the drawdown and net return that the timing rule actually produced.

**Committee monitoring and fallback.** Before continuing a pilot, refresh the expected returns. If 60/40 falls below the 6.5% estimate hurdle, stop recommending that target. Consider a smaller tilt of 55% BIL / 45% VT only if it clears the hurdle on the same refreshed inputs, the reserve and turnover tests permit the move, and the committee approves it. Under the original estimates, 55/45 returns 7.33% with 6.78% volatility. If 55/45 also fails, pause the overlay and reassess the feasible allocations. A short period of realized returns below 6.5% is evidence to review. It does not automatically trigger this estimate-based sizing decision. The 55/45 fallback is a prospective review option and is outside the historical test. A failed holdout still means reject the tested rule under the decision gate above.
