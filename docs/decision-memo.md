# Decision memo — Meridian Foundation Reserve

I used Codex to build the Streamlit app and backtest and prepare the exhibits. I chose the 10-point defensive tilt, wrote the pre-run changed-input prediction, and independently checked the ALFRED observation used for the first holdout decision.

This project is for education and does not provide investment advice.

**Committee ask.** Pilot the app as a portfolio screening and review tool. Reject deployment of the FEDFUNDS tilt under the rule tested. For the current 6.5% nominal estimate screen, review **60% BIL / 40% VT** as a research candidate. If the committee deliberately raises the screen to 7.2%, review **55% BIL / 45% VT**. Both are candidates for diligence and require approval before allocation. [Allocation results and stress checks](../output/portfolio-screen.md).

The foundation seeks CPI + 4% net of costs over rolling five years and pays $0.75 million quarterly. The annual 6.5% estimate screen uses assumed inflation of 2.5%. The reserve floor is 3%, or $3 million on the illustrative $100 million fund. The four-fund search uses annualized historical volatility and 5-point weights. Its full monthly sample, October 2021 through September 2026, gives the 60/40 candidate **6.91% estimated return and 6.04% volatility**. The 36-month estimates shift 25 points from VT to BIL, the largest feasible allocation change in the one-at-a-time checks. A reserve floor of 70% removes feasibility. The committee should review the estimates and reserve requirement before committing capital.

**Signal decision.** The rule sought to protect the reserve by moving ten points from global equities to Treasury bills during observed tightening. In the October 2025–September 2026 historical holdout, the trigger fired zero times. The overlay earned **10.09% gross / 10.08% net**, with **2.97%** maximum drawdown at monthly closes. It matched the fixed 50/50 comparison. The flipped signal earned **10.38% net** with **2.32%** drawdown. The agreed rule rejects the overlay because it trailed the flipped comparison. The quiet holdout also offers little evidence about whether the proposed shift would protect the foundation during actual tightening. A better result for the flipped comparison does not establish that it should be adopted. [Frozen rule](frozen-rule.md) · [Full holdout results](../output/holdout.md).

**Conditions and monitoring.** At each scheduled review, staff should refresh expected returns, validate reserve coverage and test the proposed trade against the 25% annual turnover limit. If 60/40 falls below the estimated return hurdle, consider the smaller tilt toward bills at 55/45 only if it qualifies on the same assumptions. Pause if no eligible allocation passes. The research range of **40%–45% VT** is conditional. A simple shock with VT down 25% and BIL flat would lose 10% at 60/40 and 11.25% at 55/45 before costs. That is an illustration of exposure near the proposed loss threshold, rather than a forecast of calendar-year loss. Before allocating, the committee should resolve liquidity capacity, look-through issuer concentration and fund eligibility, define the loss measure and escalation response, and decide whether to authorize monthly trading as an exception to annual rebalancing. Reconsider the rejected overlay only with separately registered, prospective evidence that includes a period of actual tightening and shows a benefit after costs.
